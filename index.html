ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini in 2026: the short version

By Nour, founder of Whizi. September 2026.

Most ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini arguments compare the wrong layer. Each name covers three products: a model family, a consumer app, and a developer API. The apps are close to a three-way tie at about $20 a month. The models are not, and once you price a single answer the picture gets clear fast.

The short answer: Claude wins writing and code review, ChatGPT wins debugging and structured drafts, and Gemini wins long-document work at the lowest cost per answer. All three flagships now carry 1M-token context windows, so route by task and price, not by brand.

Why the price row matters more than the benchmark row

GPT-5.5 runs roughly three times the price of the other two flagships for the same length of answer. That does not show up on a $20 app plan, because the plan hides the per-answer cost behind a usage cap. It shows up the moment you run real volume, or the moment you try to hold all three subscriptions at once and the bill lands near $60 a month.

My own routing rule, after running the same prompts through all three for most of a year: first draft with Claude, reproduce and fix bugs with ChatGPT, and hand anything over a few hundred pages to Gemini. When two of them disagree on a fact, that disagreement is the signal to go check the source, not to pick the more confident answer.

The ten-minute test that settles it

Take one real task from last week. Paste the identical prompt and source material into all three. Score each output on one axis only: how many minutes of editing stand between this and something you would send. That number is the whole comparison, and it usually surprises people who arrived with a favourite.
